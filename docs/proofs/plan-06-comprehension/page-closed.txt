Read this first

These are model predictions for a freshly applied coating on bare aluminium, not a laboratory result and not a field measurement. Intervals show parameter uncertainty only. Every PROXY index is a relative ranking, not a measurement and not a probability. Ageing appears only as a labelled scenario. Next step: make small coated aluminium samples (a coupon test) of a recipe and test them in a laboratory; these numbers only rank which recipes are worth that test.

Limits: what these models cannot say

Recipes

Best cooling: chosen for the largest modelled drop in roof temperature. Balanced: chosen to trade cooling, durability and cost evenly. Lowest cost: chosen for the lowest relative cost while still cooling the roof.

In the models, three recipes (SP-075, SP-031, SP-046) lower the peak surface temperature of an aluminium roof by about 13 to 14 K compared with bare aluminium (13.2 to 14.5 K when parameter uncertainty only is counted); the three differ by only 1.0 K, so cooling hardly separates them and cost and the PROXY indices decide between them. These are predictions for the freshly applied coating from uncalibrated models, not measurements.
Best cooling Recipe SP-075 is the cooling-priority recipe in the models: it lowers the peak surface temperature by 14.4 K [14.3, 14.5] against bare aluminium (scenario mean; parameter uncertainty only, the bare aluminium reference is nominal only), at a relative cost index of 0.64 (PROXY, relative cost units, rank 46 of 96, 1 = cheapest). Its refined variant from the optimiser is predicted at 31.2 °C peak with cost index 0.45 (an optimum of the models, nominal only).
Balanced Recipe SP-031 balances cooling, durability and cost in the models: it lowers the peak surface temperature by 13.6 K [13.4, 13.8] against bare aluminium (scenario mean; parameter uncertainty only, the bare aluminium reference is nominal only), at a relative cost index of 0.59 (PROXY, relative cost units, rank 37 of 96, 1 = cheapest). Its refined variant from the optimiser is predicted at 31.3 °C peak with cost index 0.34 (an optimum of the models, nominal only).
Lowest cost Recipe SP-046 is the cost-priority recipe in the models: it lowers the peak surface temperature by 13.4 K [13.2, 13.6] against bare aluminium (scenario mean; parameter uncertainty only, the bare aluminium reference is nominal only), at a relative cost index of 0.34 (PROXY, relative cost units, rank 1 of 96, 1 = cheapest). Its refined variant from the optimiser is predicted at 31.3 °C peak with cost index 0.34 (an optimum of the models, nominal only).

Why it works

A recipe is three thin layers on the aluminium sheet. In the models each layer has one job.

Primer
A thin sol-gel layer that bonds the coating to the aluminium and, in the models, acts as the moisture barrier for the metal.
Midcoat
A thick white layer of titanium dioxide and barium sulfate particles that scatters sunlight back, which is the layer that mainly sets the solar reflectance in the models.
Topcoat
A very thin sol-gel skin that is meant to shed water; the models rate this with a wetting PROXY index, not a measurement.

Indonesian climate

PROXY results on aluminium, with scenario ageing

The weather is modelled (NASA POWER hourly reanalysis and satellite data, 2015 to 2024), not station measurements. The roof is bare aluminium; the aged values are a scenario: the effective reflectance of a coating loses 14 to 26 % (a range from three US three-year exposure records), not an Indonesian measurement. Intervals are parameter uncertainty only (p5 to p95 over 256 draws, plus the ageing-loss range for aged recipes).

Across the five Indonesian sites the modelled heat flux into the building falls by the most in Medan (19.4 W/m² [17.4, 21.4]) and by the least in Jakarta (15.4 W/m² [13.8, 16.9]) for the cooling-priority recipe against bare aluminium, in the initial state, weighted over typical, hot and overcast days (parameter uncertainty only; weather is a model and not a station record).
Scenario ageing, not a measurement: if the coating loses 0.14 to 0.26 of its solar reflectance (a range taken from US three-year pairs, never measured on an Indonesian roof), the same benefit shrinks to 13.3 W/m² [9.7, 16.8] in Medan and 10.4 W/m² [7.6, 13.2] in Jakarta (parameter uncertainty only).

Data note

The NASA POWER values belong to a grid cell, not to the city, and the elevation of the cell is the elevation of the data. Bandung's cell is at 921.68 m (the TMYx airport file is at 740 m), so its temperatures may read 1 to 2 K cool against the city; Medan and Makassar, whose cells also lie well above city level, may read cool as well. No correction is applied.

Medan

Medan has no clear dry season: its dry months are dry only relative to its other months.

Method and limits

PROXY statement

Indices for corrosion, water repellence, delamination and cost are PROXY numbers from simple relations, not calibrated against experiments. Every PROXY index is uncalibrated: a relative ranking, not a measurement and not a probability. Intervals show parameter uncertainty only.

Method

Optics: Mie scattering of TiO2 and BaSO4 pigments in a binder, a two-flux film model and the ASTM G173 spectrum. Heat: a one-dimensional surface balance (radiation, convection, conduction into the building) over two simulated days; the second day is shown. Uncertainty: 256 Latin-hypercube draws of the uncertain material constants; intervals are p5 to p95 widened to include the nominal value (parameter uncertainty only).

The main results (recipes, explorer, live simulator, budget) describe the initial, as-applied state. Ageing appears only as a labelled scenario in the Indonesian climate section, never as a measurement.

Limits
Optics assume independent scattering (no dependent-scattering correction); no NIR binder absorption is modelled and R is held outside the 300–2500 nm range.
The wind level of 6 m/s is beyond the usual McAdams range of the convection correlation (about 5 m/s).
Every PROXY index is uncalibrated: a relative ranking number, not a measurement and not a probability.
Intervals are parameter-uncertainty only (p5–p95 over the uncertainty draws of the uncertain inputs); they exclude model-form error and are not prediction intervals for a real roof.
The sol-gel water ratio has no effect in the range 1–5 in the model.
Sol-gel condensation is taken as pH-independent in the model.
Contact angle and sliding angle are lab-verified quantities; the smooth-film model rarely reaches 110°.
Adhesion is unknown, binder compatibility is unknown, and optical constants for several materials are missing (scenario values stand in).
All values are initial (as-applied); weathering and adhesion are not modelled and ageing is not measured (the Indonesia section, when present, shows it only as a labelled scenario).

Not modelled: weathering and ageing beyond the labelled scenario, adhesion, soiling and biological growth, wind-driven rain and chloride deposition. The bare aluminium reference uses a nominal scenario value, because the sheet itself was never measured. Every PROXY index is an uncalibrated relative ranking, not a measurement or a probability.
