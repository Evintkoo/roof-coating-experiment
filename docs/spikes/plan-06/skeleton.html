Why it works: three layers, three jobs

Primer

GPTES:TEOS mole fraction 0.62:0.38. Bonds to aluminium and blocks water and ions.

Midcoat

TiO2:BaSO4 volume ratio 0.78:0.22, total PVC 0.338. White pigment scatters sunlight back out; this is where most of the cooling comes from.

Topcoat

MTES:TEOS mole fraction 0.62:0.38. Sheds water and keeps the midcoat clean.

Method and limits

Optics: Mie scattering of TiO2 and BaSO4 pigments in a binder, a two-flux film model and the ASTM G173 spectrum. Heat: a one-dimensional surface balance (radiation, convection, conduction into the building) over two simulated days; the second day is shown. Indices for corrosion, water repellence, delamination and cost are PROXY numbers from simple relations, not calibrated against experiments. Uncertainty: 256 Latin-hypercube draws of the uncertain material constants; intervals are p5 to p95 widened to include the nominal value.

What the models cannot say

Optics assume independent scattering (no dependent-scattering correction); no NIR binder absorption is modelled and R is held outside the 300–2500 nm range.

The wind level of 6 m/s is beyond the usual McAdams range of the convection correlation (about 5 m/s).

Every PROXY index is uncalibrated: a relative ranking number, not a measurement and not a probability.

Uncertainty

Intervals are parameter-uncertainty only (p5–p95 over the uncertainty draws of the uncertain inputs); they exclude model-form error and are not prediction intervals for a real roof.

The sol-gel water ratio has no effect in the range 1–5 in the model.

Sol-gel condensation is taken as pH-independent in the model.

Contact angle and sliding angle are lab-verified quantities; the smooth-film model rarely reaches 110°.

Not known or not modelled

Adhesion is unknown, binder compatibility is unknown, and optical constants for several materials are missing (scenario values stand in).

All values are initial (as-applied); weathering and ageing are not modelled here.
